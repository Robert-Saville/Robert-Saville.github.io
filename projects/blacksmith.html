Mountain & Forge

A collaborative design project for a local blacksmith business, exploring how interface design, visual systems, and thoughtful content structure can bring traditional craftsmanship into a modern digital experience.

THE PROJECT

Designing a digital experience for a traditional craft.

Mountain & Forge was a collaborative university project created for a local blacksmith business. Our team developed the design and structure for a complete website that could communicate the character of the business while providing customers with a clear, approachable digital experience.

The project required us to think beyond individual screens and consider how the entire experience would fit together. We established a visual direction, organized information, planned page layouts, and documented how the interface should function across different pages and screen sizes. My contributions focused on UI design, wireframing, website layout, and design documentation.

THE CHALLENGE

Bringing craftsmanship into a modern digital space.

Blacksmithing is a physical, handcrafted discipline. Translating that character into a digital interface meant finding a balance between a distinctive visual identity and a clear, usable website experience.

The website needed to feel professional enough to support an actual business while retaining a strong sense of personality. Visitors also needed to be able to discover the business, browse its work, understand its products and services, and navigate the different sections.

Our team approached this as both a visual and structural challenge. We worked toward a consistent system of color, typography, spacing, imagery, and layout so that the individual pages would feel like parts of one cohesive interface rather than disconnected designs.

DESIGN DIRECTION

Building a visual language around the forge.

The interface needed to connect the digital experience with the physical nature of blacksmithing while remaining clear and approachable as a modern website.

We explored how color, typography, imagery, and layout could work together to create a visual identity that felt grounded in the business without relying entirely on traditional or literal blacksmith imagery. Consistency across these elements helped establish a recognizable visual language for the interface.

TYPOGRAPHY & LAYOUT

Establishing consistency through type, spacing, and scale.

The design system uses consistent spacing, sizing, and typography rules to create a more predictable interface across the website. These shared guidelines help keep layouts aligned and establish a clear hierarchy between headings, body text, and supporting information.

WIREFRAMES & LAYOUT

Defining the structure before the final interface.

Before focusing on the final visual treatment, our team worked through the website's structure using wireframes and layout diagrams.

This stage allowed us to focus on hierarchy, content placement, navigation, and functionality without being distracted by the final visual styling. Wireframes made it easier to explore different arrangements and gave the team a shared reference point as the design developed.

DESIGN DOCUMENTATION

Documenting the experience from concept to interface.

A major project deliverable was a 46-page design document covering the website's structure, functionality, visual direction, and key design decisions.

Creating this documentation required us to think through the experience beyond individual mockups. The website's major sections and interface decisions needed to be organized and explained so that the entire team could understand the intended design.

I contributed through UI design, wireframes, website layout, and layout diagrams, helping translate the team's ideas into a documented and cohesive design direction.

THE FINAL WEBSITE

Bringing the design direction into a working experience.

After the design documentation was completed, the project moved into website implementation.

Due to the project's time constraints, the final website was implemented using Lovable based on the team's completed design documentation. The documented layouts and visual direction provided a foundation for translating the design concept into a working website.

This stage gave me another perspective on the relationship between design and implementation. Clear design documentation helps communicate the intended structure, visual hierarchy, and overall direction so that the resulting experience stays connected to the original concept.

REFLECTION

What I took away from the project.

Mountain & Forge gave me experience contributing design work to a collaborative project for an actual business. It strengthened my understanding of wireframing, UI design, layout planning, visual systems, and design documentation.

The 46-page design document was especially valuable because it required the team to think through the experience in much greater detail than individual mockups alone. It also highlighted how clear documentation can help communicate a design when it moves into implementation.

Most importantly, the project taught me how to contribute focused interface design work within a team while keeping the larger experience in mind. It reinforced the importance of consistency, communication, and thoughtful structure when bringing a digital experience together.
