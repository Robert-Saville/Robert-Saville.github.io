Ridiculous Travel Agency

Extraordinary vacations. Impossible destinations. Questionable travel decisions.

THE PROJECT

A travel agency for places you probably shouldn't visit.

Ridiculous Travel Agency is a fictional travel-booking website built around an unusual idea: what if you could book a vacation to almost anywhere, regardless of whether that destination should be reachable?

From ancient civilizations to distant planets and legendary lost cities, the project combines familiar travel-booking patterns with playful writing and imaginative destinations. I designed the experience in Framer to explore interface design, information architecture, navigation, and interactive storytelling.

Project Type

Concept Website

Platform

Framer

Focus

UI design, information architecture, interaction & prototyping

THE CHALLENGE

Making an absurd idea feel like a real booking experience.

The challenge was to make the fictional destinations entertaining without losing the familiarity of a recognizable travel website.

Visitors still need to discover destinations, compare deals, explore trip details, and move through a booking flow. The humor supports that experience rather than replacing its underlying structure.

VISUAL DIRECTION

Familiar travel design, with a few questionable destinations.

The website uses recognizable travel-site conventions to make its impossible destinations feel surprisingly bookable.

Destination discovery across extraordinary and fictional locations.

Promotional deals designed to make unusual trips feel like tempting offers.

THE EXPERIENCE

From browsing destinations to booking the impossible.

The concept extends beyond a homepage into a broader booking journey.

Dedicated destination pages provide space to explore individual trips, while deal pages present special offers. The booking page and confirmation ticket complete the intended journey from discovery to reservation.

Individual destination pages expand on the fictional travel experience.

A booking interface that follows familiar travel reservation conventions.

DESIGN PROCESS

Building a familiar experience around an unfamiliar idea.

I approached the project as a multi-page website rather than a standalone landing page, using familiar travel interfaces to make the fictional concept easy to explore.

The design process focused on establishing a consistent visual direction, organizing content across different page types, and creating a clear path from discovering destinations to exploring offers and starting a booking.

Working in Framer allowed me to build and connect the pages while experimenting with interface layouts, visual hierarchy, navigation, and interaction patterns. The writing and fictional destinations became part of the design itself, giving the site personality without abandoning recognizable booking conventions.

THE RIDICULOUS DETAILS

Every impossible vacation comes with a few rules.

Even the most absurd destinations have fine print. The Travel Guide brings the agency's humor to life through questionable advice, unusual travel rules, and warnings for travelers who probably should have stayed home.

The Travel Guide: essential advice for travelers visiting places that probably should not be on any vacation itinerary.

ITERATION & IMPROVEMENTS

A design is never finished just because it looks good in the editor.

Testing the published experience revealed layout inconsistencies that were not immediately apparent while designing in Framer.

I began investigating differences between the editor, preview, and published site, paying particular attention to page width, horizontal alignment, image positioning, and footer overflow. These issues highlighted the importance of checking a design at different viewport sizes rather than relying on a single editor view.

The next stage of refinement is to improve responsive behavior and consistency across pages, ensuring that the intended layouts hold together beyond the original design canvas. This remains an area for continued iteration rather than a problem I consider fully solved.

PROTOTYPE & REFLECTION

An experiment in making the ridiculous feel real.

This project gave me room to explore how visual design, information structure, and tone can work together across a multi-page website.

The result is a concept website and interactive prototype created in Framer. Screenshots document the intended experience and the design decisions behind it. The published version is not being presented as a production-ready booking service.
