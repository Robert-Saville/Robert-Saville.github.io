Isles of Ethor

Designing an interface system for a cozy fantasy puzzle game, exploring visual components, interaction states, and the relationship between UI design and gameplay.

THE PROJECT

Bringing the world of Ethor to life through game interface design.

Isles of Ethor is a cozy puzzle game developed as a senior capstone project at Marist University. Players take on the role of Sky, a young adventurer on a journey to reunite her fractured homeland.

Ethor was once a thriving nation powered by the magic of the Mother Crystal. Following an event known as the Fracture, the land broke apart into four separate islands. Each island retained a small piece of the Crystal's power, leaving its inhabitants struggling to maintain their environments and way of life.

Guided by the Mother Crystal, Sky sets out to explore the islands, solve puzzles, and work toward restoring her homeland. Throughout the experience, the game's interface helps players understand the systems around them, interact with the environment, and follow the game's progression.

The game was developed in Unity through a collaborative senior capstone project involving approximately 30 students. The project was divided into five specialized teams: Art, Design, Narrative, Development, and QA.

MY ROLE

Designing the interface players interact with throughout the game.

I was a member of the Art Team, where my primary contribution was designing the in-game user interface. My work focused on creating visual components and interactive elements that fit the game's fantasy setting while remaining clear and understandable during gameplay.

I worked on several different parts of the interface, ranging from larger interactive elements to smaller indicators that provide players with information and feedback. I also explored different visual states and variations to help establish a consistent interface throughout the game.

My contributions included:

  • Rune tablet interface design and interaction states.
  • Dialogue box designs and visual treatments.
  • Mana and resource indicators.
  • Reset buttons and their interactive states.
  • Environmental interaction prompts.
  • Prototype testing, quality assurance, and feedback.

In addition to my design work, I participated in prototype testing by examining the interface during gameplay, identifying issues, communicating feedback, and checking changes after fixes were made. This allowed me to see how the interface designs translated from their original concepts into the actual game.

THE INTERFACE

A collection of components designed for the world of Ethor.

The interface was designed to complement the game's fantasy setting without distracting from the environments or puzzle mechanics. I focused on making the different components feel like parts of the same system, using visual treatments and interaction states to communicate information and player actions.

Rather than focusing on a single screen, my work covered multiple interface elements that served different purposes throughout the experience. The following designs highlight the individual components, their variations, and how they contributed to the overall interface.

INTERFACE COMPONENT 01

Rune Tablet Interface

A central interface element designed to connect players with the game's puzzle mechanics.

The rune tablet was one of the central interface elements I designed. It needed to present information clearly while fitting into the game's magical visual direction. The tablet also served as an important point of interaction between the player and the game's puzzle mechanics.

Final rune tablet interface as implemented in the game.

INTERFACE COMPONENT 02

Interaction States & Design Iterations

Exploring how visual feedback can make interface interactions clearer to the player.

I explored different visual states for the interface to communicate when an element was available, selected, or being interacted with. These variations were important for providing feedback to players and making interactions easier to understand.

I also experimented with earlier versions of the rune tablet before arriving at the final direction. Comparing these iterations helped establish how the interface should look and behave within the game.

Rune tablet interaction states and an earlier design iteration.

INTERFACE COMPONENT 03

Dialogue Box Variations

Developing dialogue treatments that support the game's storytelling and visual identity.

Dialogue boxes were another important part of the interface. They needed to support the game's storytelling while remaining visually consistent with the rest of the UI. I explored different layouts and treatments to establish a style that would work alongside the game's characters and environments.

Some of these explorations included alternate dialogue box variations intended for the different islands. The idea was to give these areas their own visual character while maintaining a recognizable overall interface system.

Dialogue box layouts and alternate visual treatments.

The additional island-specific dialogue variations remained exploratory and were not implemented in the final game.

INTERFACE COMPONENT 04

Resource & Interaction Indicators

Smaller interface elements providing important information and feedback during gameplay.

Alongside the larger interface elements, I designed smaller components that supported moment-to-moment gameplay. These elements needed to communicate useful information without taking attention away from the player's surroundings.

This collection included the mana indicator, reset buttons, and the environmental interaction prompt. I also worked on the interactive states of the reset buttons so that the player could receive visual feedback when using them.

Mana indicator, reset buttons, and the "Press E to Interact" prompt.

IN-GAME IMPLEMENTATION

From individual UI elements to the finished game.

Seeing the interface in the actual game was an important part of the design process. It showed how the individual components worked alongside the environments, characters, and gameplay mechanics.

The majority of the UI components I designed were implemented in the final game. Seeing them in context helped demonstrate how the visual designs translated into functional gameplay elements and how they communicated information to the player.

TESTING & QUALITY ASSURANCE

Evaluating the interface beyond the design itself.

In addition to designing UI components, I contributed to testing the game's prototype, identifying interface issues, and communicating feedback to the team.

I also checked changes after fixes were made, helping ensure that adjustments worked as intended in the prototype. This provided an opportunity to evaluate the interface beyond its appearance and consider how the elements functioned when players interacted with them.

The experience reinforced the connection between design and implementation. Testing the interface in the actual game helped reveal issues that might not have been obvious when looking at individual designs on their own.

PROJECT CONSTRAINTS

Balancing design ideas with a large team and limited timeframe.

Developing a complete game as a senior capstone meant coordinating contributions from approximately 30 students while working within a limited academic timeframe.

With students working across five specialized teams, coordinating different responsibilities and maintaining consistency throughout the project presented challenges.

Different opinions and approaches sometimes made it difficult to maintain a shared direction. This meant that communication and coordination were important parts of moving the project forward, especially when designs needed to work alongside systems being developed by other teams.

The project timeline also meant that some additional ideas had to remain exploratory so that the team could prioritize the features and interface elements needed for the final game. Most of the UI components I designed were implemented, while the alternate island-specific dialogue box variations remained concepts rather than final game features.

WHAT I LEARNED

Designing for more than just the screen.

The project showed me that designing a game UI is about more than creating individual screens and components. It also requires communication, consistency, and collaboration across a development team.

With approximately 30 students contributing to one game, coordinating design decisions and maintaining a shared direction was challenging. Different opinions and approaches made it difficult to keep everyone aligned, and the experience showed me how important clear responsibilities, constructive feedback, and consistent communication are to a successful project.

I also learned to adapt when a project doesn't go exactly as planned. Working within a limited academic timeframe meant prioritizing the UI elements that could be completed and implemented, while recognizing that some additional concepts would remain exploratory.

Most importantly, the project helped me understand the value of testing designs in context. A component might look good on its own, but it still needs to function clearly and consistently during actual gameplay.

REFLECTION

Bringing design, implementation, and collaboration together.

Isles of Ethor gave me the opportunity to contribute to a complete game development project while exploring interface design, interactive feedback, and quality assurance. Being able to see my designs move from individual concepts into an implemented game helped me better understand the relationship between visual design and functionality.

The experience also gave me a better understanding of designing as part of a larger team, adapting to project constraints, and evaluating interface elements within their intended environment. It was an opportunity to contribute to a collaborative game while developing practical experience creating UI for an interactive experience.
