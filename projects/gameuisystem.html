Isles of Ethor

Designing a collection of visual and interactive UI components for a cozy fantasy puzzle game, exploring interface design, visual feedback, and how game UI supports the player experience.

THE PROJECT

Bringing the world of Ethor to life through game interface design.

Isles of Ethor is a cozy fantasy puzzle game developed as a senior capstone project at Marist University. Players take on the role of Sky, a young adventurer exploring a fractured homeland and working to restore its former glory.

Developed in Unity, the game was a collaborative project involving approximately 30 students across five specialized teams: Art, Design, Narrative, Development, and QA. My work focused on designing the in-game interface and contributing to prototype testing.

MY ROLE

Designing the interface players interact with throughout the game.

As a member of the Art Team, my primary contribution was designing the game's user interface. I created visual components and interactive elements that complemented the fantasy setting while remaining clear and understandable during gameplay.

My contributions included:

  • Rune tablet interface design and interaction states.
  • Dialogue box designs and visual treatments.
  • Mana and resource indicators.
  • Reset buttons and interactive states.
  • Environmental interaction prompts.
  • Prototype testing, quality assurance, and feedback.

I also participated in prototype testing, identifying interface issues, communicating feedback, and checking changes after fixes were made.

THE INTERFACE

A collection of components designed for the world of Ethor.

The interface was designed to complement the game's fantasy setting while keeping information and player interactions clear. Across different components, I explored visual treatments, layouts, and interaction states to create a consistent interface.

INTERFACE COMPONENT 01

Rune Tablet Interface

A central interface element connecting players with the game's puzzle mechanics.

The rune tablet was one of the primary interface elements I designed. It needed to present information clearly while fitting into the game's magical visual direction.

INTERFACE COMPONENT 02

Interaction States & Design Iterations

Exploring visual feedback and different approaches to the interface.

I explored variations to communicate when interface elements were available, selected, or being interacted with. I also experimented with earlier versions of the rune tablet before arriving at the final design.

Rune tablet interaction states and an earlier design iteration.

INTERFACE COMPONENT 03

Dialogue Box Variations

Creating dialogue treatments that support the game's storytelling and visual identity.

I explored different dialogue layouts and visual treatments to establish a style that worked alongside the game's characters and environments. Additional concepts included alternate dialogue designs for individual islands, giving each area its own visual character.

Dialogue box layouts and alternate visual treatments.

The additional island-specific variations remained exploratory and were not implemented in the final game.

INTERFACE COMPONENT 04

Resource & Interaction Indicators

Smaller interface elements providing information and feedback during gameplay.

I also designed supporting elements for moment-to-moment gameplay, including the mana indicator, reset buttons, and environmental interaction prompt. These components needed to communicate useful information without distracting from the player's surroundings.

Mana indicator, reset buttons, and the "Press E to Interact" prompt.

TESTING & QUALITY ASSURANCE

Evaluating the interface beyond the design itself.

Alongside my design contributions, I participated in prototype testing to evaluate how the interface functioned during gameplay.

This involved identifying UI issues, communicating feedback to the team, and checking changes after fixes were made. Testing the interface in the actual game helped reveal issues that weren't always apparent when viewing individual designs on their own.

REFLECTION

Designing for more than just the screen.

Isles of Ethor gave me the opportunity to contribute to a complete game development project while designing interface components for an interactive experience. Seeing my work move from individual concepts into the finished game helped me better understand the relationship between visual design and functionality.

Working alongside a large team also taught me the importance of communication, collaboration, and adapting designs to project constraints. Most importantly, the experience reinforced the value of testing interface designs in their intended environment, where clarity and functionality matter just as much as appearance.
